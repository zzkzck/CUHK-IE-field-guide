# Course Intelligence

> Official outlines describe the course.  
> Course reviews describe the damage pattern.

## 0. Why This Section Exists

Official course outlines are necessary.

They are also incomplete.

They tell you the syllabus, assessment scheme, prerequisites, and learning outcomes. They rarely tell you the things students actually need to know before committing their semester:

- whether the course is secretly workload-heavy
- whether lectures are useful or ceremonial
- whether tutorials matter more than lectures
- whether past papers are essential
- whether the final exam is a massacre
- whether assignments consume far more time than the units suggest
- whether the grading scheme creates hidden pressure
- whether the course is a GPA risk
- whether taking it with other heavy courses is a bad idea

That missing information is not decoration.

It affects grades, sleep, time allocation, semester planning, and sometimes mental health.

This section exists to record that missing information.

Not politely.

Usefully.

> Study the course before you study for the course.

## 1. The Hong Kong Course Review Tradition

Hong Kong students have always asked the real questions:

> 伏唔伏?  
> Chur 唔 chur?  
> Attendance check 唔 check?  
> Past paper 有冇用?  
> Final 殺唔殺?  
> Grade 得唔得?  
> Professor 伏唔伏?  
> 邊個 sem take 冇咁死?

This culture exists because official information is not enough.

A course outline may say “programming assignment”.  
It will not say “this assignment eats your entire reading week”.

A course outline may say “final exam 50%”.  
It will not say “the final rewards people who farmed past papers from Week 5”.

A course outline may say “tutorial”.  
It will not say “the tutorial is the only place where the course makes sense”.

A course outline may say “recommended textbook”.  
It will not say “nobody opens it”.

So students ask seniors.

They trade warnings.

They build informal intelligence networks because the formal system leaves blind spots.

This is not gossip by default.

It becomes gossip when it has no structure, no evidence, and no survival value.

This section keeps the tradition, but cleans it up.

Less noise.  
More signal.

Less “呢科好伏”.  
More “this course becomes dangerous if you underestimate the labs”.

Less emotional dumping.  
More operational warning.

## 2. What This Section Is For

This section is for answering questions that actually affect how students survive a course:

- What does this course really demand?
- What kind of student does it punish?
- What background does it assume?
- What should be started early?
- What can be safely deprioritised?
- What should never be ignored?
- Which assessments are the real killers?
- Is the workload steady or deadline-spiky?
- Is the difficulty conceptual, mathematical, programming-based, writing-based, or just badly managed?
- Is this course worth taking in a heavy semester?
- What should a student know before walking in?

A useful review does not merely say:

> This course is hard.

It says:

> This course is hard because the assignments are open-ended, debugging-heavy, and poorly aligned with lecture examples. Start early or suffer.

A useful review does not merely say:

> The lecture is useless.

It says:

> The lecture mostly repeats slides. Tutorial examples and past papers are better learning inputs.

A useful review does not merely say:

> GPA killer.

It says:

> The grading is exam-heavy, the final is time-pressured, and small conceptual gaps become expensive.

Labels are not enough.

Explain the trap.

## 3. What This Section Is Not For

This section is not for worshipping courses.

It is not for protecting official narratives.

It is not for pretending every course is well-designed.

It is not for calling every difficult course “valuable”.

It is not for saying “it depends” until the warning becomes useless.

But it is also not for lazy ranting.

Do not write:

> This course is trash.

Write:

> This course has a serious mismatch between teaching pace and assessment difficulty. Students who only follow lectures may be underprepared for the final.

Do not write:

> The professor is useless.

Write:

> The lectures were not useful for exam preparation because they mostly repeated slides and gave few worked examples.

Do not write:

> Don’t take this course.

Write:

> Do not take this course in a semester where you already have two heavy programming courses unless you can start assignments early.

Be harsh on structure.

Be precise about damage.

Do not waste words on insults.

## 4. How to Read These Reviews

Do not ask only:

> Is this course easy?

That is a weak question.

Ask:

> What does this course punish?

Some courses punish late starts.  
Some punish weak maths.  
Some punish bad coding workflow.  
Some punish passive lecture attendance.  
Some punish poor memory.  
Some punish slow handwriting.  
Some punish weak group coordination.  
Some punish students who do not read announcements.

Different punishments require different strategies.

A course being “chur” is not enough information.

Find out why.

- Chur because of weekly labs?
- Chur because of long assignments?
- Chur because of unclear teaching?
- Chur because of proof-heavy content?
- Chur because of final exam pressure?
- Chur because of group project risk?
- Chur because every small deadline matters?

If you do not know the type of danger, you cannot plan around it.

> “伏” is a warning label.  
> It is not an analysis.
